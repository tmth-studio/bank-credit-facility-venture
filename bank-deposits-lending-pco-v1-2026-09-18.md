## 2d · Level the line

### 2d.1 · The job as stated

A business bank takes money from businesses, holds it, moves it on instruction, and lends money to businesses against a promise to repay. One balance sheet funds the second job with the first.

### 2d.2 · The delivery test

The question is not whether the two jobs share a balance sheet. They do. The question is whether the market delivers them the same way today.

| | Hold and move money (the account) | Lend money (loans and overdrafts) |
|---|---|---|
| Made | An account on a core system after identity and financial-crime checks | A credit decision after assessment of financials, credit history and security |
| Sold | Opened at the start of the relationship, mostly digital, often with a free period | Applied for when a need arises; 47% of applications succeed (S2) |
| Delivered | Instant access; the account runs itself | A facility drawn once, or an overdraft attached to the account |
| Paid for | A monthly fee after the free period, plus the interest the bank earns on the balance | Interest at a margin over base, plus an arrangement fee |
| The gate | Is this business who it says it is, and is it safe to serve | Will this business repay |
| Who is refused | 0.2%–11.4% of applications declined; 1.0%–6.9% of accounts terminated a year (S6) | 43% of applicants declined; 48% of no-employee applicants (S2) |

Four of the four delivery rows differ. Two different gates refuse two different populations. **INFERENCE.** The two jobs are two conventional forms. Each has its own critical limiting operation. Challenge one's single-case gate would fail on a PCO that spans both.

### 2d.3 · The levelled cases

| | Case A — the account | Case B — the credit facility |
|---|---|---|
| Job | Hold a business's money and move it on instruction | Lend a business working money against a promise to repay |
| Levelled case | The business current account, with the deposit it carries | The term loan and the overdraft, taken together |
| Next-broader case excluded | Consumer-style accounts for the unregistered sole trader | Asset finance and invoice finance |
| The delivery difference | The sole trader banks on a personal account today, made under consumer rules with no business identity check | Asset and invoice finance are made against the asset or the invoice, not against the business, and sold by specialist lenders |

**Why loans and overdrafts level to one case.** The market makes both with the same credit decision, on the same financials and security, priced the same way. Success rates track business size in the same way for both: 37% for loans and 47% for overdrafts overall (S2). The overdraft is delivered through the account, but it is made by the credit gate, not the identity gate.

### 2d.4 · The case for this venture — delegated

Case A is Kept. The 17 September Kept business case shows £59 of the £92 realised per customer is group deposit interest; Kept is the deposit line re-formed. A second run on it would be a restatement.

Case B is open. The runner proposes Case B as this venture's PCO. Sections 3 to 6 carry both cases so the principal can reverse the choice without a rerun.

## 2c · The insider's lens — drafted ⚠ ASSUMED, public sources only

**What the line sells, to whom, at what price.** Business current accounts to UK businesses up to about £25m turnover ⚠ UNVERIFIED, priced at a monthly fee after a free period, plus the interest earned on balances. Loans and overdrafts to the same base, priced at a margin over base rate plus an arrangement fee. The four largest UK banks hold 85% of business accounts (S9).

**Who cannot buy.** On the account: businesses that fail identity or financial-crime checks, and businesses the bank judges uneconomic to serve, which the FCA records is lawful outside basic bank accounts (S6, S7). On credit: the smallest and youngest businesses. Success falls from 94% for businesses with 50 to 249 employees to 39% for those with none (S2). Reasons given for decline: current performance, credit rating, lack of security (S2).

**Typicality.** On accounts the bank is the market default: the big four hold 85% (S9). On lending the high-street form is no longer the majority of the flow. Challenger and specialist banks wrote 60% of gross SME bank lending in 2025, up from 39% in 2012 (S1). The high-street lenders together wrote £17.5bn against £68bn for all banks (S1, S4). **INFERENCE.** The conventional form — a credit decision on financials, rating and security — is shared across both groups. The challengers deliver the same form faster. The typicality check passes on form and fails on share. Record both.

## 5 · Scale target — mode 3

Scale is the incumbent's volume on the line. Public floor for the high street as a whole: £17.5bn gross SME lending in 2025, £4.6bn in the fourth quarter (S4). The bank's share is a red field: [gross SME lending a year £]. The required-revenue line of section 1 is tested against that volume, not against a penetration of the market.

**The inverted-trap guard (RD-023).** Mode 3 sets no reach or cut target. The venture is not asked to grow the book. It is asked to show what the conventional form costs at the incumbent's own volume. Then it shows what a form that reaches the excluded would cost at the same volume.

## 6 · PCO statement — Case B, delegated

**Who.** UK businesses with fewer than 50 employees that need working money and would repay it: the bank's borrowing customers, plus the applicants the credit gate refuses and the founders who lend to their own business instead.

**The condition they face.** The conventional form decides credit on financials, a credit history and security. The smallest businesses cannot supply them. Success falls from 94% to 39% across the size range (S2). One in five SMEs put in personal money because they had to (S2). Half of those who apply are declined; a further 5% do not apply because the process deters them.

**Why it has the right financial shape.** Scale is the incumbent's own book, so the revenue line does not depend on a share of a market. The excluded rows count about 1.5m businesses: 1.25m self-funders plus 285,000 deterred, before the declined are added ⚠ UNVALIDATED. The public proxy for current buyers is 28% of 5.7m, about 1.6m (S2, S8). The excluded population is about the same size as the served one. Whether their loss clears the required price is the R2 question.

**PCO statement, Case A, held in reserve.** UK businesses that need to hold and move money: the bank's account holders, plus those refused or removed on financial-crime or cost grounds, plus the unregistered sole traders on personal accounts. Kept covers the current buyers; the excluded rows are new.
