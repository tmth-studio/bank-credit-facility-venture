# A business bank's credit facility line — the counterfactual at the F1 gate · 21 September 2026

**What this file is (registry row "Counterfactual costed (F1 gate)", RD-030).** The C1–C3 form launched as it stands, with requirements R4 to R10 handled the conventional way. It is costed with the conventional lines the later challenges attack, each as a band. A named real business is costed from its public record and every C1–C3 floor line is checked against it. The corrected floor, if any, is propagated to C1–C3 under VA-103 before C4 opens.

**Venture:** Case B — the credit facility line of a large UK SME bank, Path C mode 3. The bank is not named; every figure only the bank holds is a red field `⚠`. **Unit:** £ per £1,000 of average drawn balance, per year, at scale. **Written by:** the runner, at the C3 close, object-after 23 September 2026 17:00.

**The answer, first.** The counterfactual's floor is ⚠ 69–169 per £1,000 a year. Against the selected customer's ceiling (142.50 discounted) it fails the 25 per cent gate at the worst corner (−15.7 per cent) and passes at the best (313 per cent). The named instance, iwoca, runs at ⚠ £119–149 per £1,000 a year on its public record — inside the counterfactual band and above the C1–C3 floor. No C1–C3 line is re-costed: the two lines where the instance's figure is larger (funding; people) are design choices the C1 record made and names. **The floor 54–106 stands. What R4–R10 must do is stated as one number: the conventional additions must fall to ⚠ £8 per £1,000 or less at the high corner for the 25 per cent gate to hold.**

## 3 · The named instance — iwoca

**Why iwoca.** The closest public business to the counterfactual. It offers an unsecured revolving credit line to UK small businesses, underwritten from bank-account and accounting data, at a scale near £1bn of loans. It acquires its customers the conventional way (brokers, embedded partners, marketing) because it has no account base of its own. Square Loans (Block) is the closer mechanism but sits inside a payments company whose lending headcount and capital are not separable in its filings — a stated absence for the operating lines; its loss rate is used as a public reference below.

**Cost per £1,000 a year, from the record.** Cost = revenue − profit = ⚠ £143m − £24m = £119m. Over a book of ⚠ £0.8–1.0bn: **⚠ £119–149 per £1,000 a year.** This is inside the counterfactual band (69–169) and above the C1–C3 floor (54–106). The instance therefore supports the counterfactual as costed and does not, on its own, contradict the form's floor — because the form removes the operations the instance performs, and that is what section 4 checks line by line.

One sentence is added to the C3 record's checks table: "Counterfactual costed — PASS — written; floor unchanged."

## 5 · What the counterfactual tells R4–R10

- The form's advantage over the counterfactual is ⚠ 15–63 per £1,000 a year. Every pound of it is a conventional line a later challenge must remove by design, not by efficiency.
- The largest line is R7's (⚠ 6–35). A bank with its own account base has a gateway the instance lacks — the account. Whether that closes R7 at ⚠ £8 or less is R7's question, by number only.
- R6 and R9 add nothing: the C1 move already did their conventional work. R4, R5, R8 and R10 together are ⚠ 9–28.
- The instance's revenue per £1,000 (⚠ £143–179) is above the selected customer's ceiling low corner (150). A form that spends like the instance cannot price below the tariff. That is the counterfactual's lesson in one line.
