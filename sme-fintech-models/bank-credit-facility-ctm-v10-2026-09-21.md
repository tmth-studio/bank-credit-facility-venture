# Business bank credit facility line — customer transformation map (CTM), v10, at C10

**Venture:** the credit facility line of a large UK SME bank (Case B — term loan and overdraft), Path C mode 3 — see `../bank-credit-facility-c10-v1-2026-09-21.md`. **v10 written at R10** (21 Sep 2026), runner-drafted under the propose-and-confirm rule. **v9**, **v8**, **v7**, **v6**, **v5**, **v4**, **v3**, **v2** and **v1** are retained unchanged as the C9 to C1 records. **Update mode:** only the rows R10 adds or changes are new (marked *C10*, in bold); every other row is carried from v9 with its trace and its bold removed. R10 adds one component — PC-2, levelled-line funding and the standby route: the pool's drawn balances are funded by the pool's own credit balances at the measured deposit cost under a signed funding term, the aggregate drawn book is capped at ⚠ 10% of the pool's credit balances, and a revolving master trust on the outcome ledger is kept ready without issuance. The block removed: the line's funding priced by a convention outside the line — at C9 the funding line was booked at the pool's deposit cost and nothing held it there. The customer's journey does not change; the funding behind it does. No component is removed. What follows in this paragraph is the v9 text, carried: R9 adds one component — WP-6, the outcome ledger and calibration: the pool's record of every limit set, every funded event and its clearance or loss, kept by cohort with the multiple in force, from which the rule's base multiple and seasoning schedule are re-fitted yearly. No external score enters the limit rule. The block removed: the rule's parameters set from outside its own outcomes — at C8 the base multiple was the Stage 0 back-test's and the schedule a set of Tier 4 figures, so a rival with a generic rule started level on parameters. The customer's journey does not change; the parameters behind it do, once a year. No component is removed.

**Defined terms.** As in v1: *flow*, *standing limit*, *draw*, *receipts share*, *account line*. New at C2: *Standard tariff* — the published interest rate and fees a bank charges on a business overdraft that is not individually negotiated. *Migration* — the one-time move of a customer's existing arranged overdraft, and any balance drawn on it, into the standing limit and a draw with a receipts share. *Price condition* — the rule that the all-in price of the limit per £1,000 drawn stays below the standard-tariff cost the customer paid before migration; a condition, not a price — the price and its structure are C6's. New at C3: *Shadow limit* — the standing limit the rule computes and records for an account without the customer being able to draw on it. *Back-test* — the rule run over the bank's own past flow and compared with the loss actually taken. *Release* — the event at which a shadow limit becomes drawable; by stage, never by application. *Overlay* — the extra loss allowance finance books while a model is unproven. *Trial band* and *replica band* — the kill bands for the first instance and for each repeat (VA-163). New at C4: *Shortfall* — the day a scheduled payment (direct debit, standing order or a payment the customer has set up) exceeds the available balance; the bank sees it before it settles. *Limit history* — the standing limit shown beside the twelve months of the customer's own shortfalls it would have covered, each with the date, the amount, the balance that day and the limit that day; computed from the stored daily limit and the back-test (WP-5). *Placement* — the limit history put in front of the customer on the shortfall day, in the account, with nothing sent. New at C5: *Use block* — the routine disruption the C1 form itself introduced: the draw was a customer act at a cue (the shortfall) that instant payment channels give no moment to perform. *Default funding* — the account rule that a payment exceeding the available balance is met from the standing limit, within the limit, with nothing for the customer to do; the same mechanism an arranged overdraft already uses. *Acceptance* — the customer's one-time agreement to the limit term at release (for the migrated customer, the modifying agreement under CCA 1974 s.82 where the agreement is regulated). *Draw ahead* — the explicit draw retained as an option: the customer moves money from the limit to the balance before a payment. *Funded event* — one payment met from the limit under default funding; the unit the account records and the billing unit C6 will price. *Available balance* — the credit balance plus the undrawn standing limit, shown as one figure. *Switch-off* — the customer's option to turn default funding off for the account; a payment that then exceeds the balance is returned as it is today. *Reversal* — a customer's repayment of a funded event within three days of it, read as a signal that the funding was unwanted. New at C6: *The charge* — the price of the limit: interest accrued daily on the drawn balance at one rate (14.0% simple a year; band ⚠ 13.5–14.25%), no fee. *Accrual* — the daily addition to the accrued-charge ledger (drawn balance × rate ÷ 365); the accrued charge is not added to the drawn balance and bears no interest. *Collection* — the first slice of each receipts share clears the accrued charge; the remainder repays the drawn balance; no debit is made for the charge. *Representative example* — the CONC 3.5.5R illustration shown wherever the rate is shown. *Calendar debit* — the conventional charging event (interest and fees debited on a fixed date), the block R6 removes. New at C7: *Bank feed* — the account-information service the bank must give an authorised provider on the customer's consent (PSRs 2017 reg. 69; the CMA Order 2017), through the Open Banking standard. *Balance response* — what the bank returns on each balance call: the balance, its type and an optional list of credit lines (amount; type: Available, Credit, Emergency, Pre-Agreed, Temporary; a flag stating whether the balance figure includes it). *Pre-agreed credit line* — the element of the balance response that carries the released standing limit, with the flag set to *not included* so the balance figure stays the actual balance. *Connected account* — an account with a limit that a platform or an accountant reads through a bank feed (⚠ 45–70% of accounts with a limit). *Forecast shortfall* — a shortfall the customer's or the accountant's cash-flow forecast shows ⚠ 2–12 weeks ahead of the bank's scheduled-payment file. *Access block* — the reason a convinced customer who needs nothing to use the limit still seeks a facility elsewhere: the limit is absent from the seat where the shortfall is first foreseen; the block R7 removes. New at C8: *Cleared event* — a funded event whose drawn balance has returned to zero through the receipts share, with no receipt-free accrual older than ⚠ 90 days at clearance. *Seasoning* — the count of cleared events on the account and the months since the first; the rule's second input. *Seasoned limit* — the standing limit computed from the flow and from the account's seasoning: the multiple of flow rises with cleared events from the base at six months of flow to the full multiple at ⚠ 36 months and ⚠ 12 cleared events; average uplift on the book ⚠ 15–30%. *Seasoning schedule* — the published rule that maps seasoning to the multiple; a parameter of the limit rule, validated yearly by model risk. *Store of value* — what accumulates on the account through use of the limit that a customer loses by moving the account and no rival can supply on day one: the account's own record on the limit; the block R8 removes is the rule's single input. New at C9: *Outcome ledger* — the pool's record of every daily limit set (the multiple in force, the flow band, the release stage), every funded event and its clearance, stall or write-off, kept by cohort (seasoning × flow band × stage); per-account rows are the customer's (the seasoning line), cohort aggregates are the bank's. *Calibration* — the yearly re-fit of the base multiple by flow band and the seasoning schedule from the outcome ledger; validated by model risk, approved at the release-track sitting; the published schedule is its current output. *Generic rule* — a limit rule whose parameters are set once from public or bought data and not re-fitted from the pool's own outcomes. *Held-out cohort* — accounts kept on the previous parameters for one period so the re-fit's loss gain can be read. New at C10: *Transfer price* — the rate the bank's treasury charges a lending book for its funds; a convention. *Measured deposit cost* — the interest the bank paid on the pool's credit balances in the period ÷ those balances; ⚠ 1.5–3.0% a year. *Levelled-line funding* — the pool's drawn balances funded by the pool's own credit balances at the measured deposit cost under a signed term, capped at ⚠ 10% of the balances. *Standby route* — a revolving master trust on the outcome ledger, documented and maintained without issuance, opened when the transfer price exceeds its all-in cost for two consecutive quarters.
